Crushing Debt

How interest works against you — and two proven ways to fight back.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Interest: the price of borrowing

When you borrow, you pay back more than you borrowed. The extra is interest, shown as an APR (annual percentage rate).

A $5,000 credit card balance at 24% APR, paying $150/month, takes about 4½ years to clear and costs over $3,300 in interest. Pay only $100/month and the interest eats the whole payment — the balance never goes down.

Avalanche vs. Snowball

Always pay the minimum on everything. Then put every extra dollar toward one debt:

  • Avalanche: highest interest rate first. Saves the most money.
  • Snowball: smallest balance first. Quick wins keep you motivated.

The best method is the one you'll stick with.

Try it: compare both in the Debt Payoff planner.

Good debt vs. bad debt

Not all debt is equal. A low-rate mortgage or a sensible student loan can build long-term value. High-interest debt for things that lose value — like credit card shopping — is the kind to eliminate fast.
