Filing Your First Tax Return

Whether you need to file, which forms to gather, free ways to file, and how to get your refund.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Do you have to file?

For 2026 income (the return you file in 2027), a single person generally has to file if their income was more than the $16,100 standard deduction. That's true even if a parent claims you as a dependent, as long as your income was all from a job.

Different rules apply if you had more than $1,350 of investment income (like interest) as a dependent, or $400 or more of self-employment profit (see Side Hustles).

Even if you don't have to file, file anyway if income tax was withheld from your paychecks. Filing is the only way to get that money back.

Source: IRS, Rev. Proc. 2025-32 and Publication 501. The IRS's Interactive Tax Assistant on IRS.gov checks your situation.

Gather your forms

  • W-2 from every employer. They must send it by January 31.
  • 1099-NEC if a business paid you $2,000 or more as a freelancer in 2026, and 1099-INT if a bank paid you $10 or more in interest.
  • 1098-T if you paid college tuition (it can unlock education credits).
  • Your Social Security number and your bank routing and account numbers for your refund.

No form for some income? You still report it.

File for free

  • IRS Free File: free guided software if your income is under the IRS limit ($95,000 or less, per IRS.gov in November 2026). It opens in January 2027.
  • Free File Fillable Forms: free at any income if you're comfortable doing the forms yourself.
  • VITA: free help from IRS-certified volunteers for people who generally make about $70,244 or less. Find a site with the VITA Locator on IRS.gov.

Watch for "free" ads that charge you later for a state return or extras. The IRS's own Direct File program has ended.

Source: IRS.gov Free File and free tax preparation pages (checked November 2026).

Getting your refund

The IRS began phasing out paper refund checks in September 2025. To get a refund now, you generally need to give your bank's routing and account numbers for direct deposit. File electronically and most refunds arrive within 21 days.

The deadline for 2026 returns is April 15, 2027. If you owe, pay by then: an extension gives you more time to file, not to pay.

The IRS doesn't start contact by text, email, or social media asking for personal or bank information. Messages promising a refund are a common scam (see Spotting Scams).

Source: IRS, 2026 filing season announcement.

After you file

  • Keep a copy of your return and forms for at least three years.
  • Got a huge refund, or owed a lot? Adjust your W-4 with your employer so next year's paychecks withhold closer to the right amount.
  • Most states also have their own income tax return. Many free options file both.
