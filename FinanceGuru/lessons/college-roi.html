Is College Worth It?

Think about a degree like an investment: cost, payoff, and alternatives.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

On average, yes

In 2024, full-time workers with a bachelor's degree earned a median of $1,543/week (~$80,000/yr), vs $930/week (~$48,000/yr) with only a high school diploma. Their unemployment rate was lower too: 2.5% vs 4.2%.

But averages hide huge differences. What you study and what you pay matter a lot.

Major matters

Among recent grads (ages 22–27), median pay ranges from about $90,000 (computer engineering) and $85,000 (chemical and aerospace engineering) down to around $40,000–46,000 for some arts, education, and social science majors.

Some majors also have high underemployment — grads working jobs that don't need a degree.

Explore: compare all 73 majors in the College hub.

Cost matters just as much

Average 2025–26 tuition & fees: $4,150 at community college, $11,950 in-state public, $31,880 out-of-state, $45,000 private (before grants).

Smart moves: file the FAFSA, consider 2 years at community college, stay in-state, and try to borrow no more than your expected first-year salary. Many skilled trades and 2-year healthcare careers pay very well too.
