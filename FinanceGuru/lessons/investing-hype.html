Investing Hype: Crypto, Meme Stocks & Finfluencers

Tell a real investment from a social-media pitch, and see why FOMO gets expensive.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Where young investors learn

In a 2023 survey of U.S. investors ages 18 to 25, 48% said they learn about investing mainly through social media. Half had made an investment driven by FOMO (fear of missing out), and their most common investment was crypto.

Social media can be a fine place to start learning. It's a risky place to take orders.

Source: FINRA Investor Education Foundation and CFA Institute, Gen Z and Investing (May 24, 2023).

Finfluencers often get paid

Many "finfluencers" are paid to promote a coin, app, or course. The FTC requires paid endorsements to be disclosed, but not everyone does it.

In 2022 the SEC charged Kim Kardashian for promoting a crypto token on Instagram without saying she was paid $250,000 to do it. She paid $1.26 million to settle.

Before you act on a tip, ask: Who's paying them? What do they get if I buy? Are they licensed? You can look up brokers and advisers for free on FINRA's BrokerCheck.

Crypto: big swings, few protections

Crypto prices can move wildly. Bitcoin lost about three-quarters of its value between November 2021 and November 2022. That same month, the crypto exchange FTX collapsed and customers couldn't get their money out.

Crypto isn't FDIC-insured. If an exchange fails or your account is hacked, there may be no one to make you whole.

If you buy any, keep it to a small slice of money you could afford to lose completely.

Meme stocks and pump-and-dumps

Meme stocks jump on online buzz, not business results. People who buy late often buy near the top.

In a pump-and-dump, promoters hype a cheap stock or coin, sell their own shares to the people they convinced, and the price crashes.

In 2025, people reported losing more than $7.9 billion to investment scams, with a typical (median) loss over $10,000. Investment scams that started on social media cost $1.1 billion. Red flags: guaranteed returns, "act now," private group chats, and pressure to recruit friends.

Sources: FTC consumer alert (April 16, 2026) and press release (April 27, 2026), 2025 Consumer Sentinel data.

Practice: spot the red flags in the Spot the Scam game.

The boring plan that works

  • Build your emergency fund first.
  • Invest for the long run in low-cost, diversified index funds, automatically, every month.
  • Keep money you'll need within about five years out of the stock market.
  • Treat any hype bet as entertainment money, sized like a concert ticket, not your savings.

If something is a sure thing, ask why a stranger is telling you about it.
