Investing 101

Stocks, index funds, and why boring is beautiful.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

What investing means

Investing is buying things you expect to grow in value or pay you income over time — like a small slice of a company (a stock) or a loan to a government or company (a bond).

Investing has risk: values go up and down. That's why it's for money you won't need for 5+ years.

Index funds: own the whole haystack

Instead of searching for the one winning stock (the needle), an index fund buys a little of hundreds or thousands of companies at once.

  • Diversified — one company failing barely matters.
  • Low cost — fees often under 0.1% per year.
  • Hard to beat — most professional stock pickers underperform the index over long periods.

Use tax-advantaged accounts

Many countries offer retirement accounts with tax perks. In the US, a 401(k) through work (grab any employer match — it's free money!) and an IRA you open yourself are the big ones.

Remember: this is education, not personal financial advice. Consider talking to a licensed fiduciary advisor for your specific situation.
