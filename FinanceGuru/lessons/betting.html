The Math of Sports Betting & Gambling

Why the house always wins over time, and how betting apps keep you playing.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Betting is everywhere now

In a 2025 Pew Research survey, 31% of adults ages 18 to 29 said they had bet money on sports in the past year, more than any other age group.

In 2025, Americans bet about $167 billion with legal sportsbooks, which kept about $17 billion of it (American Gaming Association). That's about 10 cents of every dollar bet.

You must be 21 to bet on sports in most states where it's legal.

How odds hide the house edge

A typical bet is listed at −110: you risk $110 to win $100. If the game is a true 50/50, you'd win $100 half the time and lose $110 half the time.

That's an average loss of $5 per $110 bet, a 4.5% edge for the sportsbook. Win half your bets and you still lose money.

Every gambling game is built this way. American roulette keeps 5.26% on average, and the odds of hitting the Powerball jackpot are 1 in 292,201,338.

Try it: play the Beat the Book game, then see what happens when 10,000 players make the same bets.

Parlays: big payouts, bigger edge

A parlay combines several bets, and all of them must win. A 4-leg parlay of −110 bets pays about 12-to-1, but four coin flips in a row only happen 1 time in 16.

Fair odds would pay 15-to-1, so the house keeps about 17% on average, nearly four times the edge on a single bet. Sportsbooks promote parlays because they make more money from them.

Built to keep you playing

  • "Bonus bets" and promos often come with rules that require you to bet much more before you can withdraw.
  • Near misses (one leg short of a big parlay) feel like you almost won, so you play again.
  • The gambler's fallacy: after five losses, a win isn't "due." Each bet is independent.
  • Chasing losses, betting more to win back what you lost, is a warning sign of a gambling problem.

If you choose to gamble

  • Treat it as entertainment you pay for, like a concert ticket, not a way to make money.
  • Set a limit you can afford to lose before you start, and never use money for bills, rent, or debt.
  • Use the app's deposit limits and time-outs.

If betting stops being fun, free and confidential help is available 24/7 from the National Problem Gambling Helpline: call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), or chat at ncpgambling.org/chat.
