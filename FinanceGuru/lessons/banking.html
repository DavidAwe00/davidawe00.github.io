Banking Basics: Accounts, Interest & Fees

Pick the right accounts, earn real interest, and stop paying fees you don't have to.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Checking vs. savings

  • Checking is for everyday money: your paycheck lands here, and your debit card, bills, and payment apps pull from it.
  • Savings is for money you're not spending this month, like your emergency fund or a goal. It pays interest, and keeping it separate makes it harder to spend by accident.

Banks and credit unions both offer these. Credit unions are member-owned nonprofits that often charge lower fees.

Where you save matters

In September 2026, the average savings account in the U.S. paid just 0.37% a year (FDIC national rate). Top online high-yield savings accounts paid around 4% (Bankrate).

On $5,000 for a year, that's about $19 of interest versus about $200, for the same money and the same safety.

Deposits are insured up to $250,000 per person, per bank, per ownership category, by the FDIC at banks or the NCUA at credit unions. Money sitting in a payment app may not be insured unless it's moved to a bank.

Rates change often, and some top rates are introductory. Check the current APY before you open an account.

The fees that eat your money

Average bank fees in 2025 (Bankrate):

  • Overdraft: $26.77 each time you spend more than you have and the bank covers it.
  • Insufficient funds (NSF): $16.82 when a payment bounces instead.
  • Out-of-network ATM: $4.86 per withdrawal, counting both your bank's fee and the ATM owner's.

Overdraft on a $5 coffee can turn it into a $32 coffee, and several can hit in one day.

Practice: find every fee on a bank statement in the Fee Hunter game.

How to pay $0 in fees

  • Choose a no-fee account (no monthly fee and no minimum balance), or meet the waiver rules, often a direct deposit.
  • Skip overdraft coverage for debit and ATM. By law, banks need your opt-in to charge overdraft fees on those, so a declined card costs $0.
  • Turn on low-balance alerts in your bank's app.
  • Use your bank's ATM network, or get cash back at a store checkout.
