Budgeting Basics

Tell your money where to go instead of wondering where it went.

What a budget really is

A budget isn't a punishment. It's a plan for your money made before you spend it.

Without a plan, money tends to leak away on small things. With one, you decide what matters most to you — and fund that first.

The 50/30/20 rule

A simple starting point for your take-home (after-tax) pay:

  • 50% Needs — rent, groceries, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments.
  • 30% Wants — eating out, streaming, hobbies, travel.
  • 20% Savings & extra debt payments — your future self.

The 50/30/20 guideline was popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in All Your Worth (2005).

Try it: the Budget Planner splits your income for you — or practice in the Envelope Budget game.

Pay yourself first

The most powerful budgeting habit: move money to savings the day you get paid, automatically. Then spend what's left.

If you wait to save "whatever is left over," there usually isn't anything left over.
