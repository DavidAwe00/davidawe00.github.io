Side Hustles & Self-Employment Taxes

Gig money is real income. Here's how it's taxed and how to keep more of it.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

W-2 job vs. 1099 gig

At a regular W-2 job, your employer withholds income tax from each paycheck and pays half of your Social Security and Medicare tax.

As an independent contractor (delivery, rideshare, freelancing, reselling, tutoring), nobody withholds anything. You're the business, so you handle your own taxes, including both halves of Social Security and Medicare.

All of it counts, form or no form

Starting in 2026, a business only has to send you a Form 1099-NEC if it paid you $2,000 or more in the year (up from $600). Payment apps and marketplaces send a 1099-K only after $20,000 and 200 transactions.

But those are reporting rules for businesses, not tax-free limits. All self-employment income is taxable, and you must file a return once your net self-employment earnings reach $400.

Self-employment tax

On your profit, you pay 15.3% self-employment tax (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare, figured on 92.35% of profit), plus regular income tax. You can deduct half of the self-employment tax.

Together, that often adds up to 25%–30% of your profit. The safest habit: move that share into savings every time you get paid.

Find your number: the side hustle tax calculator shows exactly how much to set aside.

Deductions: keep receipts and log miles

You're taxed on profit: income minus business expenses. Common ones: supplies, the business share of your phone, platform fees, and equipment.

If you drive for work, the IRS standard mileage rate is 72.5¢ per mile for January–June 2026 and 76¢ from July 1, 2026. Driving 3,000 business miles at 76¢ deducts $2,280. Log every trip (date, miles, purpose) as you go; an app makes it easy.

Pay as you go

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, the IRS expects quarterly estimated payments, generally due in April, June, September, and January. Skipping them can mean penalties. If you also have a W-2 job, raising your withholding on your W-4 can cover it instead.

A side hustle also counts as earned income, which means you can contribute to a Roth IRA with it.
