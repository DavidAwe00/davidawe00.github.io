Retirement Accounts 101

401(k), Roth IRA, HSA — the tax-advantaged containers for your investments.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

The account is the container

An account like a 401(k) or IRA isn't an investment itself — it's a container with tax perks. Inside, you choose investments, like index funds.

Those perks are a big deal: over decades, avoiding taxes on growth can add up to tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The big three (U.S., 2026 and 2027 limits)

  • 401(k) through work: up to $24,500/yr in 2026, $25,500 in 2027. Many employers match part of what you put in — that's free money.
  • IRA you open yourself: up to $7,500/yr (same in 2026 and 2027).
  • HSA with a high-deductible health plan: up to $4,400 (self) or $8,750 (family) in 2026; $4,500 or $9,000 in 2027.
Compare all accounts: see the account guide in the Investing Hub.

Roth vs. traditional

Traditional: skip taxes now, pay them when you withdraw in retirement.

Roth: pay taxes now, then the money grows and comes out tax-free.

Rule of thumb: if you expect a higher tax rate later (common for young people early in their careers), Roth often wins.
