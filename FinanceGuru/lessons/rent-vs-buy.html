Renting vs Buying a Home

Mortgages, equity, and the hidden costs of owning.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

How a mortgage works

A mortgage is a loan to buy a home, usually repaid over 15 or 30 years. In mid-October 2026, the average 30-year fixed rate was about 7.40% (Freddie Mac).

Early payments are mostly interest; over time, more goes to principal — the part that builds your equity. On a 30-year loan at that rate, you can pay more in interest than the house originally cost.

See it: the mortgage calculator shows where every year's payments go.

More than the mortgage

Owners also pay property tax (about 0.89% of value per year on average in 2024), insurance, maintenance, and often mortgage insurance (PMI) with less than 20% down. Closing costs typically add about 2%–5% upfront.

HUD considers households that spend more than 30% of income on housing "cost-burdened."

Rent isn't 'throwing money away'

Renters skip maintenance, property tax, and big upfront costs — and can invest the down payment. Buyers build equity and lock in their payment.

Which wins depends mostly on how long you stay: it can take several years for a buyer to outrun closing and selling costs.
