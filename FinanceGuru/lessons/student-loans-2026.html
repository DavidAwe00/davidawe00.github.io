Student Loans: The 2026 Rules

RAP vs. the Tiered Standard Plan — what changed and how to choose.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Two plans for new loans

Starting July 1, 2026, new federal student loans come with two repayment choices:

  • Tiered Standard Plan: fixed payments. The term depends on your balance — 10 years under $25,000, up to 25 years for $100,000+.
  • Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP): payments based on your income.

The undergraduate federal loan rate for 2026–27 is 6.52%, fixed for the life of the loan.

How RAP works

RAP charges 1%–10% of your yearly income (adjusted gross income), divided by 12 — the percentage rises one point for each $10,000 above $10,000, maxing out at 10% above $100,000. It's reduced $50 per dependent, and the minimum is $10/month.

Two built-in helps: interest your payment doesn't cover is waived, and the government adds up to $50/month toward principal if your payment barely dents it. Whatever's left after 30 years is forgiven (possibly taxable).

Compare: try both plans with your numbers in the Credit & Debt hub.

Choosing wisely

Tiered Standard usually costs less in total if you can afford the payment. RAP protects you when income is low or unsteady — but as your income rises, so does your payment, and paying for decades can add up.

Either way: you can always pay extra with no penalty. Paying off high-rate loans faster is a guaranteed return equal to the interest rate.
