Taxes 101: Brackets & Paychecks

Why a raise never shrinks your paycheck, and what FICA is.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Brackets are buckets

The U.S. has seven federal income tax rates in 2026, from 10% to 37%. But your income isn't all taxed at one rate.

Picture buckets: the first dollars fill the 10% bucket, the next fill the 12% bucket, and so on. Each bucket's rate applies only to the dollars inside it. So moving into a higher bracket only taxes the extra dollars at the higher rate — a raise can never lower your take-home pay.

See it: slide your income in the bracket buckets visual.

Deductions shrink taxable income

Before the brackets apply, you subtract deductions. Most people take the standard deduction: $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly in 2026.

Pre-tax 401(k) contributions and health premiums also lower your taxable income — which is why saving for retirement through work costs less than it looks.

FICA: the other paycheck tax

On top of income tax, almost every paycheck pays FICA: 6.2% for Social Security (on wages up to $184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% for Medicare. Your employer pays a matching amount.

Your W-4 form tells your employer how much income tax to withhold. A giant refund isn't free money — it's your own money, returned without interest.
