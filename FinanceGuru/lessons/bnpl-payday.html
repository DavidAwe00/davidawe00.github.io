Buy Now, Pay Later & Payday Loans

Easy money at checkout can get expensive fast. Learn to see the real price.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

Buy now, pay later

"Pay in 4" plans split a purchase into four payments over about six weeks, usually with no interest if you pay on time. In the Fed's survey of 2025, 16% of adults used one.

The catch: 21% of users paid a late fee or got hit with an overdraft or bounced-payment fee. Problems were most common when people used it for groceries or food delivery. Juggling several plans makes it easy to lose track of what's due.

Source: Federal Reserve, Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (2025 data).

Payday loans

A payday loan is a small, short loan due on your next payday. A typical fee is $15 per $100 for two weeks. That sounds small, but the CFPB says it equals an APR of almost 400%, compared with about 22% on the average credit card.

The trap is the rollover: if you can't repay, you pay another fee to push the due date back, again and again, while still owing the full amount.

Turn any fee into an APR

APR = fee ÷ amount borrowed × 365 ÷ days

Example: a $60 fee on a $400 loan for 14 days: 60 ÷ 400 × 365 ÷ 14 ≈ 391%.

Try it: compare loans in the fee-to-APR calculator.

Better options when cash is tight

  • Your emergency fund: even $500 saved keeps you out of most of these traps.
  • Credit union payday alternative loans: small loans with APRs capped at 28% (NCUA rules).
  • Ask your employer for a pay advance, or a biller for a payment plan or due-date change.
  • If you use buy now, pay later, use it for one planned purchase at a time, with autopay from an account that has the money.
