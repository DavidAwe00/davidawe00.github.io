Insurance 101: Health, Car, Renters & Life

Pay a little now so one bad day can't wipe you out.

Start the interactive lesson →Leer en español →

How insurance works

You pay a premium (a monthly or yearly price). In return, the insurer pays for big losses that would be hard to cover yourself: a hospital stay, a car crash, a stolen laptop.

Thousands of people pay in, a few have bad luck, and the premiums cover them. The rule of thumb: insure against losses you can't afford, and handle small ones from your emergency fund.

Health insurance words

  • Premium: what you pay every month, even if you never see a doctor.
  • Deductible: what you pay for care each year before the plan starts sharing costs. The average single deductible at work was $1,886 in 2025 (KFF).
  • Copay / coinsurance: your share after the deductible, such as $25 a visit or 20% of the bill.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you pay in a year (not counting premiums). For 2026, plans can't set it above $10,600 for one person.

Most plans cover preventive care like checkups and vaccines at no cost, and you can usually stay on a parent's plan until age 26.

Practice: choose the cheaper plan for real-life years in the Plan Picker game.

Car insurance

  • Liability pays for damage and injuries you cause to others. Nearly every state requires it.
  • Collision fixes your car after a crash; comprehensive covers theft, hail, or hitting a deer. Lenders usually require both if you have a car loan.

Young drivers pay the most because they have the most crashes. Ways to lower it: stay on a family policy, keep good grades (many insurers give a discount), drive safely, and choose a higher deductible if you have the savings to cover it.

Renters insurance

Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your stuff. Renters insurance covers your belongings after fire, theft, or water damage, pays for a place to stay if you're displaced, and includes liability if someone gets hurt at your place.

It averages about $170 a year, around $14 a month (Insurance Information Institute, from NAIC data).

Life and disability insurance

Life insurance matters once someone depends on your income, like a partner or children. Term life covers a set number of years and is far cheaper than whole life, which mixes insurance with a savings account.

Disability insurance replaces part of your paycheck if illness or injury stops you from working. For most young workers, their ability to earn is their biggest asset. Check whether your job offers it.
