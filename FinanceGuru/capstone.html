🎓 Your First Year After Graduation

The course capstone. Pick where you live, how you get to work, what goes into your 401(k), where you save, how you spend, and how you use credit. Then see what twelve months of those choices add up to. Results are saved in this browser only.

How this works

Each month your take-home pay lands in checking and pays rent, transport, essentials ($700: groceries, phone, utilities, and a health premium), and wants. Anything over a $500 cushion moves to savings unless you keep it in checking. A short month pulls from savings, then your credit card, then overdrafts. In month 7 a $1,200 surprise bill arrives.

Real figures: 2026 federal tax brackets, standard deduction, and FICA (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, SSA); average credit card APR 22.36% (Federal Reserve G.19, Aug 2026); savings rates 0.37% national average (FDIC, Sept 2026) and about 4% at top online banks (Bankrate); overdraft fee $26.77 (Bankrate 2025); renters insurance about $170 a year (Triple-I from NAIC data); average new car $50,562 (Kelley Blue Book, Sept 2026); new-car loans about 7% APR (Edmunds, Q3 2026); car insurance $2,098 a year and depreciation $4,422 a year (AAA, Your Driving Costs 2026, scaled to the car's price); housing at 30% of gross pay (HUD) and the 20/4/10 car guideline.

Illustrative: the $50,000 salary, $3,000 starting savings, 4% flat state tax, the 4% employer match, rents, the used car's price and 9% APR, $200 a month for gas and maintenance, the $2,000 card limit, and the bonus.
