🪣 How tax brackets really work

Think of brackets as buckets. Your income fills the 10% bucket first, then the 12% bucket, and so on. Only the dollars inside each bucket are taxed at that bucket's rate — so moving into a higher bracket never taxes all your income at the higher rate.

📝 Filling out a W-4 (new job checklist)

The W-4 tells your employer how much federal tax to withhold. Since 2020 it has no "allowances" — just five steps. Only Steps 1 and 5 are required.

1️⃣

Personal info

Name, address, Social Security number, and filing status. Required.

2️⃣

Multiple jobs or working spouse

Skip if you have one job. Otherwise check the box or use the IRS estimator so you don't under-withhold.

3️⃣

Claim dependents

If you qualify for credits like the child tax credit, enter them here to lower withholding.

4️⃣

Other adjustments

Optional: other income (like interest), extra deductions, or extra tax to withhold each paycheck.

5️⃣

Sign it

Unsigned forms don't count. Required.

💡

Big refund? Not a win.

A large refund means you lent the government money interest-free all year. Owing a lot means you under-withheld. Aim close to zero — the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator helps.

🆕 New deductions for 2025–2028

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act added temporary deductions you can take even if you don't itemize.

💵

No tax on tips

Deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips per year (tax years 2025–2028), if your job is on Treasury's list of tipped occupations. Phases out above $150,000 of modified AGI ($300,000 joint). Social Security and Medicare tax still apply.

⏱️

No tax on overtime

Deduct up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint) of qualified overtime — only the extra "half" of FLSA-required time-and-a-half pay. Same income phase-out. Payroll taxes still apply.
