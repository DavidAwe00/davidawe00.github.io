🔧 The true cost of owning a home

The mortgage is just the start.

🧾

Closing costs

Lender fees, title insurance, appraisal, and more — typically about 2%–5% of the loan or price, due at closing on top of your down payment.

🛡️

Mortgage insurance (PMI)

Usually required on conventional loans with less than 20% down. By law it generally ends automatically once your balance is scheduled to hit 78% of the home's original value.

🏛️

Property tax

Averaged about 0.89% of home value per year nationally in 2024 — but ranges from a fraction of that to more than double, depending on where you live.

🔨

Maintenance & repairs

Roofs, water heaters, and HVAC systems wear out. Many owners budget 1%–2% of the home's value each year.

🏘️

HOA fees

Condos and many neighborhoods charge monthly association dues — check them before you buy.

🔑

Low down payment options

FHA loans allow 3.5% down with a credit score of 580+, but add mortgage insurance. Many states and cities also run first-time buyer programs.

Buying is usually better when…

  • You'll stay at least several years — long enough to outrun closing and selling costs.
  • The full monthly cost (not just the mortgage) fits comfortably — HUD considers households paying more than 30% of income on housing "cost-burdened."
  • You still have an emergency fund after the down payment and closing costs.
