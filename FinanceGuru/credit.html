🎓 Student loan repayment plans (2026 rules)

For federal loans made on or after July 1, 2026, borrowers choose between two plans: the Tiered Standard Plan (fixed payments, term set by balance) and the income-based Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP). Compare them with your numbers.

How the plans work
  • Tiered Standard: fixed monthly payments over a term based on what you owe — under $25,000: 10 years; $25,000–$49,999: 15 years; $50,000–$99,999: 20 years; $100,000+: 25 years. No forgiveness.
  • RAP: pay 1%–10% of your AGI a year (1% at $10,001–$20,000, rising one point per $10,000, up to 10% above $100,000), divided by 12, minus $50 per dependent; at least $10/month. Interest your payment doesn't cover is waived, and if a payment cuts principal by less than $50, the government adds up to $50 so principal falls by at least what you paid. Any balance left after 360 payments (30 years) is forgiven — and may be taxable.
  • This model assumes on-time payments, a single filer, and your income growth assumption. Use the official StudentAid.gov Loan Simulator before making decisions. Parent PLUS loans have different rules.

✅ Smart credit habits

⏰

Never miss a payment

Set autopay for at least the minimum. Payment history is the single biggest factor in a FICO score.

📉

Keep utilization low

Use a small slice of your limits — under 30% is a common guideline, and lower is better.

💯

Pay cards in full

Pay the statement balance every month and you'll typically owe no interest at all.

🕰️

Keep old accounts open

A longer credit history helps. A no-fee card you've had for years is worth keeping.

🧐

Check your reports

Get free reports from all three bureaus at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute errors.

🛑

Apply sparingly

Each application can trigger a “hard inquiry.” Space them out, especially before a big loan.
