How this is calculated
  • Degree path: 4 years of tuition minus grants; part-time pay during school; salary starts at the major's early-career median at 22, rises to its mid-career median by 40, then holds flat. Loan payments over 10 years after graduation.
  • No-degree path: your 18–21 earnings estimate; then $40,000 at 22–27 (NY Fed median for high-school-only workers, 2025), rising to $48,360 by 40 (BLS 2024 median for high-school graduates age 25+).
  • Living costs are left out because you pay them on either path. Figures are pre-tax and not adjusted for inflation or investment returns. Some of the earnings gap reflects differences between people who do and don't go to college, not just the degree itself.

✂️ Seven ways to cut the cost of college

📝

File the FAFSA — every year

It unlocks federal grants, work-study, and many school scholarships. Lots of families who'd qualify never apply.

🏫

Try the 2 + 2 route

Two years at community college ($4,150/yr average tuition) then transfer. Same diploma at the end.

📍

Stay in-state

Public in-state tuition averages $11,950 vs $31,880 out-of-state — about $80,000 saved over four years.

🎯

Look at net price, not sticker price

After grants, the average private college student pays far less than the $45,000 list price. Use each school's net price calculator.

⚡

Earn credit early

AP, IB, CLEP, and dual-enrollment classes can knock out a semester or more.

📏

Follow the debt rule

Try to borrow no more in total than your expected first-year salary. Federal loans before private ones.

⏱️

Finish in four

A fifth year costs another year of tuition and a year of lost salary.
