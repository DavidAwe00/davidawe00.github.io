🎈 Inflation: the invisible tax

Prices rise a little almost every year, so a dollar buys less over time. Since 1928, U.S. inflation has averaged about 3% a year — enough to cut a dollar's buying power in half roughly every 23 years.

🏦 Where to invest: account types (U.S., 2026)

The account is the container; the investments go inside it. Tax-advantaged containers can be worth hundreds of thousands over a lifetime.

A common order of operations

  1. Starter emergency fund ($1,000 or one month of expenses).
  2. 401(k) up to the employer match — it's an instant 50–100% return.
  3. Pay off high-interest debt (anything above ~8%, like credit cards).
  4. Full emergency fund (3–6 months).
  5. Roth IRA (or traditional) — up to $7,500 in 2026.
  6. HSA if you have a high-deductible health plan.
  7. Max the 401(k), then a taxable brokerage account.

Limits: IRS Notice 2025-67. Income limits apply to Roth IRA contributions. This is education, not personal advice.
